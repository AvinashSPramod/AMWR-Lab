Advanced Mechanism & Wearable Robotics Lab

We envision robotics that seamlessly integrates with human needs, capabilities, and environments. Our research explores the design and development of human-centric robotic systems, bringing together system design, engineering, and robotics to address challenges in physical assistance, rehabilitation, and human augmentation. While wearable robotics forms a central part of our research, our interests extend to soft robotics, field robotics, and service robotics, driven by the diverse challenges and opportunities at the intersection of humans and machines.

About the Laboratory

Led by Dr. Avinash S Pramod in the Department of Industrial Design at the National Institute of Technology (NIT) Rourkela, the Advanced Mechanism and Wearable Robotics (AMWR) Lab focuses on the design and development of human-centric robotic systems through advanced mechanism design, engineering, and experimental validation. Our research spans wearable robotics, physical human-robot interaction, assistive and rehabilitation systems, and emerging areas of robotics.

Core Research Pillars

THEME 01

Mechanism Synthesis & Kinematics

Closed-loop linkage optimization, self-aligning anatomical joints, and passive spring-cam-pulley transmissions engineered for lightweight, unpowered assistance.

THEME 02

Industrial & Assistive Exoskeletons

Upper-limb and lower-back wearable systems designed to offload musculoskeletal strain during overhead lifting and repetitive industrial stoop tasks.

THEME 03

Soft Cable-Driven Exosuits

3D geodesic cable-routing models and active anchor-point reconfiguration validated on instrumented phantom benches and human subjects.
