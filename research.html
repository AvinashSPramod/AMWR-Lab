Hardware-first investigation of wearable mechanisms, kinematic compatibility, and physical human-robot interaction.

1. Multi-Degree-of-Freedom Wearable Shoulder Mechanisms

Human shoulder kinematics involve coupled glenohumeral and scapulothoracic motion that frequently causes joint misalignment in rigid exoskeletons. We synthesize compact, compliant, and kinematically compatible shoulder mechanisms evaluated on instrumented multi-DOF shoulder phantom testbeds.

  • Kinematic synthesis for wide range-of-motion overhead tasks
  • Misalignment sensitivity mapping and parasitic force reduction
  • Hardware prototyping and phantom-based mechanical validation

2. Passive Dual-Tunable Industrial Back-Support Exoskeletons

We develop unpowered lower-back exoskeletons that store and return elastic energy during stoop and squat lifting. By incorporating four-bar linkages and tunable pulley offset/angle mechanisms, the assistive torque magnitude and peak torque phase can be customized to user anthropometry and task load.

  • Independent tuning of peak assistive torque magnitude and engagement angle
  • Lightweight machined structural links for industrial durability
  • Validated via optical motion capture, surface EMG, and dual force plates

3. Reconfigurable Cable-Driven Soft Exosuits

Soft exosuits transmit tensile forces across biological joints via Bowden cables and textile anchors. We model 3D geodesic cable trajectories over anatomical surfaces and develop active cable-path reconfiguration mechanisms to adapt moment arms across multi-planar motions.

  • 3D geodesic cable routing and capstan friction characterization
  • Active anchor-point reconfiguration for upper- and lower-limb assistance
  • Stepwise validation from rigid precision phantoms to soft-tissue human trials
